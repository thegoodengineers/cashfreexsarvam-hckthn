What these tests taught us

  • On an unreadable photo the reader invents data. On lad4 Sarvam returned account 0123456789012345, a GSTIN and a 2023 date that are not on the bill. It scored that account 0 confidence, but our reader treated a 0 as fully sure (0 or 1 is 1 in Python). We fixed it: a 0 now marks the field unsure. The bill was never at risk, because code compares every account with the one on file.
  • A model reads, code decides, and that is why it held. Every wrong read we saw was caught by a cross-check (account on file, GSTIN, arithmetic, amount in words), not by trusting the model's confidence.
  • Scripts are not the hard part. Tamil and Hindi names, Tamil and Hindi amounts in words, Devanagari digits and lakh notation were all read right. On Devanagari digits Sarvam was unsure (0.5) of a correct total, so the owner is asked once instead of the bill being paid on a guess.
  • One systematic slip: the letter O inside Hope's GSTIN is read as zero on every Hope bill. Harmless here, since the vendor is then matched by name, but it is why the GSTIN is never the only key.
